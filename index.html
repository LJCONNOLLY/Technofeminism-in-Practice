A Hands-On Feminist Exploration of Digital Technologies

This graduate seminar examines the gendered and racialized assumptions embedded in digital infrastructures, from algorithms to interfaces. Through interactive storytelling, collaborative ethnographies, and hands-on platform audits, we'll engage with technofeminist theory and practice.

Learn More View Modules

About This Course

🔍 Focus

Critically examining the gendered and racialized assumptions in digital infrastructures through interactive storytelling and multimedia presentations.

💡 Core Ideas

  • Feminist Science & Technology Studies (STS)
  • Algorithmic bias and discrimination
  • Embodied interaction and situated knowledges
  • Design justice and accountability

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Apply feminist and intersectional frameworks to analyze digital technologies
  • Identify and articulate gendered and racialized patterns in algorithms and platforms
  • Conduct collaborative digital ethnographies
  • Audit digital platforms for bias and discrimination
  • Reimagine more equitable digital futures

🛠️ Course Format

Asynchronous Elements:

  • Collaborative digital ethnographies
  • Multimodal reflections (audio, video, zine)
  • Interactive case studies and visualizations
  • Podcast and audiobook engagements

Major Project: Platform Audit

Throughout the semester, you'll audit an algorithm or digital platform (e.g., TikTok, ChatGPT, LinkedIn, Duolingo, Canvas LMS) for gendered and racialized patterns. Your findings will be presented as a multimodal report in the format of your choice.

Video Essay Audio Documentary Interactive Zine Traditional Paper Data Visualization
