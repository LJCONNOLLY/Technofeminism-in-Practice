Glossary of Key Terms

Essential concepts in technofeminist theory and practice.

Algorithmic Bias

Systematic and repeatable errors in computer systems that create unfair outcomes, such as privileging one category over another in ways that reflect existing social inequalities.

Algorithmic Oppression

Safiya Noble's concept describing how search engines and algorithms reinforce and amplify racism, particularly anti-Black racism. Goes beyond individual bias to structural discrimination.

Cyborg

In Donna Haraway's work, a hybrid of organism and machine that challenges binary thinking and offers possibilities for feminist politics beyond essentialist identity categories.

Data Colonialism

The appropriation of human life through data extraction practices that mirror historical colonial logics of resource extraction and exploitation.

Design Justice

An approach to design that centers marginalized communities' expertise, redistributes power in design processes, and explicitly works toward liberation.

Embodied Interaction

Understanding human-computer interaction through the lens of embodiment—how our physical, social, and cultural experiences shape technology use and design.

Feminist STS

Feminist Science and Technology Studies: an interdisciplinary field analyzing how gender, race, class, and other social categories shape scientific knowledge and technological development.

Ghost Work

Mary Gray and Siddharth Suri's term for hidden human labor that makes "automated" systems function, from content moderation to data labeling.

Glitch Feminism

Legacy Russell's framework using the "glitch"—errors, refusals, and disruptions in digital systems—as sites of resistance and world-making for marginalized identities.

Intersectionality

Kimberlé Crenshaw's framework for understanding how race, class, gender, and other identity categories intersect to create unique experiences of discrimination and privilege.

Platform Capitalism

Economic model where digital platforms extract value by facilitating interactions between users while controlling the infrastructure and data.

Situated Knowledges

Donna Haraway's epistemological framework arguing that all knowledge is produced from particular social positions, and that acknowledging this enables more objective understanding.

Standpoint Theory

Feminist epistemological position that those who are marginalized can produce knowledge that is more objective because they must understand both dominant and marginalized perspectives.

Technological Redlining

Digital discrimination that mirrors historical redlining practices, systematically denying or degrading services to communities based on race, class, or geography.

Technofeminism

Approach to technology that applies feminist analysis—examining power, justice, intersectionality—to design, development, and use of digital systems.

The New Jim Code

Ruha Benjamin's term for how new technologies can reproduce and reinforce racial hierarchies under the guise of neutrality and progress.
